## 🚨 Critical Assumptions (Test These First)

### Assumption 1: Reddit is best acquisition channel
**Test:** Week 1-2 (2 Reddit posts, track upvotes + installs)  
**Validation:** 10+ installs per post OR 50+ upvotes  
**If False:** Pivot to X, Discord, or YouTube Shorts

### Assumption 2: Players will pay $0.99 for cosmetics
**Test:** Week 8 (add tip jar, track purchases)  
**Validation:** 5%+ conversion rate (5 buyers per 100 players)  
**If False:** Lower price to $0.49 or add more value

### Assumption 3: Consulting can generate $1K/month in 4 weeks
**Test:** Week 1-4 (submit 40 Upwork proposals, track responses)  
**Validation:** 1-2 clients onboarded at $500-$1,500  
**If False:** Pivot to Fiverr (lower rates, faster wins)

### Assumption 4: 20% Day 7 retention is achievable
**Test:** Week 5-8 (track 100+ players, measure retention)  
**Validation:** 20%+ return after 7 days  
**If False:** Fix core loop (interview churned players)

### Assumption 5: 50 beta testers = enough feedback
**Test:** Week 1-4 (acquire 50 testers, analyze feedback quality)  
**Validation:** Actionable feedback from 30%+ of testers  
**If False:** Need 100+ testers (scale Reddit efforts)

## 💡 Mental Models for Decision-Making

### Model 1: Traction > Features
**Question:** "Should I build this feature or acquire 10 more players?"  
**Answer:** Always choose traction. Features mean nothing without users.

### Model 2: Data > Opinions
**Question:** "I think X will work better than Y."  
**Answer:** Test both. Let metrics decide. Your opinion doesn't matter.

### Model 3: Revenue > Validation
**Question:** "Should I focus on proving retention or generating income?"  
**Answer:** Both. Consulting funds development. Retention validates product.

### Model 4: Fast Feedback > Perfect Execution
**Question:** "Should I polish this or ship it and get feedback?"  
**Answer:** Ship scrappy. Iterate based on real feedback, not assumptions.

### Model 5: Systems > Heroics
**Question:** "Should I work 80 hours this week to catch up?"  
**Answer:** No. Build systems (automation, templates, SOPs) that scale without burnout.
